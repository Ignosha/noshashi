Explainer

What NOSHASHI is

NOSHASHI is a read-only compliance and risk workspace for the XRP Ledger. Paste the address of a token issuer, a wallet or a payment, and it reads the ledger directly to tell you who controls that asset, what its market can really absorb, what a payment actually delivered, and whether a counterparty is one you should not touch.

Every answer names the validated ledger it came from and carries a SHA-256 receipt, so it can be shown to a colleague, an auditor or a regulator and checked by them. NOSHASHI never holds keys, never signs and never moves funds. It is used in the browser, as a desktop app for macOS, Windows and Linux, and through an API.

Our objective is simple: no institution should hold or move an XRPL asset without first reading what the ledger already says about it.

The problems it solves

  1. I

    An issuer can freeze or claw back a balance, and its website will not tell you.

    NOSHASHI reads the issuer's account flags and signer list, so you know whether it can freeze holders, claw back, gate who may hold, or act with a single key.

  2. II

    An order book can show depth that is not there.

    It counts only the offers whose owners hold the funds at that ledger, so the depth you see is the depth that can trade. See Figure 3.

  3. III

    A payment can deliver far less than it states.

    Partial payments are legal on the XRP Ledger. NOSHASHI reports the delivered amount, the figure that should be credited.

  4. IV

    A sanctioned or scam counterparty can reach you in a few hops.

    Deposits and withdrawals are screened against OFAC-listed senders up to three hops back, the scam registry, lookalike addresses and counterfeit tokens.

  5. V

    An auditor asks what you saw, and on which date.

    Each reading can be exported as an evidence report with the ledger index and a fingerprint anyone can verify, now or months later.

How to use it

Four steps, the first of them free and without an account.

  1. Paste an address

    An issuer, a wallet, or a payment's transaction hash, in the free tools below or in the workspace. Nothing to connect, nothing to sign.

  2. Read the answer

    Each line is a ledger fact with a plain explanation: what the issuer can do, what can really fill, what was delivered. Anything that could not be read is shown as not read, never as a pass.

  3. Keep the receipt

    Decide GO, HOLD or NO-GO against your own versioned policy, and keep the SHA-256 receipt or export an evidence report for the file.

  4. Watch what you hold

    Set issuer alerts on Telegram or Discord, or call the Asset Risk API from your own onboarding and treasury systems.

Method

What is read

Validated ledger state only: an issuer's account flags and signer list, trust lines, offers and what their owners hold, and the delivered amount recorded on each payment. Nothing pending, nothing inferred.

Every answer names the ledger index it was read at and carries a SHA-256 receipt over its contents, so anyone can re-read the same ledger and check it.

What is not

Off-ledger facts are outside the ledger and outside these readings: reserves and backing, redemption terms, attestations, and the issuer's legal identity. A domain on an issuer account is self-declared.

Nothing here says an asset is safe, legitimate or a good investment. These are records of ledger facts, not opinions.

Read it yourself
