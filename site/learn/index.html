PART 1 · THE XRP LEDGER

1 · How the ledger agrees on the truth

ABOUT 6 MINUTES

The XRP Ledger (XRPL) is a public database, run since 2012, that keeps one shared record of who owns what. Thousands of servers around the world keep identical copies. Anyone can read it. Nobody, including its creators, can quietly change it.

Ledgers, one after another

The record moves forward in steps. Every 3 to 5 seconds the network produces a new ledger: a complete snapshot of every account and balance, plus the transactions that changed things since the last one. Each ledger has a number, its ledger index, which only ever goes up. At the time of writing it is past 107 million.

Ledger 107,207,970→ 3–5 s →Ledger 107,207,971→ 3–5 s →Ledger 107,207,972

Consensus: how strangers agree

There is no mining. Instead, special servers called validators compare the transactions they have seen and vote. Each server trusts a list of validators it believes will not collude, called its UNL (Unique Node List). When at least 80% of trusted validators agree on the same set of transactions, that ledger becomes a validated ledger. From that moment it is final: it cannot be reversed or edited.

Ledgers exist in three states:

  • Open / current: the ledger still collecting transactions. Its contents can change.
  • Closed: no more transactions, but not yet agreed.
  • Validated: agreed and final. This is the only kind NOSHASHI reads.

THINK OF IT LIKE

A notebook that thousands of accountants keep at once. Every few seconds they compare their newest page. When 80% of the ones you trust have written the same page, it is glued into the book forever and given a page number.

Why this matters for NOSHASHI

Because validated ledgers never change, a reading taken "at ledger 107,207,971" can be checked by anyone, at any time, by asking any server for that same ledger. Every NOSHASHI result carries its ledger index for exactly this reason.

KEY IDEAValidated means final. A ledger index is a timestamp nobody can argue with.

2 · Accounts, keys and reserves

ABOUT 6 MINUTES

Addresses

An account is a place on the ledger that can hold XRP and tokens. It is identified by an address that starts with r and is 25 to 35 characters long, for example Bitstamp's issuing account rvYAfWj5gh67oV6fW32ZzP3Aw4Eubs59B. There is also a newer X-address format that packs an address and a destination tag into one string.

Keys

Control of an account comes from a secret key. The secret (or seed, usually starting with s) generates a key pair: the private key signs transactions, the public key lets everyone check the signature. Anyone with the secret controls the account completely, so it must never be shared. NOSHASHI never asks for one: it only reads public data.

Reserves

To stop people filling the shared ledger with junk, every account must keep some XRP locked as a reserve: a base reserve to exist (currently 1 XRP) plus an owner reserve for each object it owns, such as a trust line or an offer (currently 0.2 XRP each). Validators can vote to change these amounts. Reserved XRP cannot be spent, which is why NOSHASHI distinguishes a balance from what is actually spendable.

What an account records

FIELDWHAT IT HOLDS
BalanceIts XRP, in drops (1 XRP = 1,000,000 drops).
SequenceA counter that goes up with each transaction; starts at the ledger index when the account was created, not at zero.
FlagsOn/off settings, including the issuer powers in lesson 5.
OwnerCountHow many objects it owns, which sets its owner reserve.
RegularKeyAn optional second key (lesson 6).
DomainAn optional web domain the owner claims; can be verified against a file on that domain.
TransferRateFor issuers, the fee charged when holders transfer its tokens (lesson 5).

A COMMON MISREADING

An account's Sequence is not how many transactions it has made. It starts at the ledger index of the account's creation. NOSHASHI's Provenance screen corrects for this when it estimates account age.

KEY IDEAWhoever holds the key owns the account. Balance minus reserve is what can actually move.

3 · Transactions and what their results mean

ABOUT 5 MINUTES

Every change to the ledger is a transaction: a signed instruction such as Payment, OfferCreate (place a trade), TrustSet (open a trust line) or AccountSet (change a setting). Each one pays a tiny fee in XRP, called the transaction cost, which is destroyed rather than paid to anyone. When the network is busy the cost rises; NOSHASHI shows this as fee pressure.

Life of a transaction

  1. The owner signs it with their key and submits it to a server.
  2. The server gives a tentative result. This is not final.
  3. Validators include it in a ledger that becomes validated.
  4. Its final result is now fixed in the transaction's metadata.

Result codes

PREFIXMEANINGEXAMPLE
tesSuccess. The transaction did what it said, within its rules.tesSUCCESS
tecFailed, but still recorded and the fee was charged.tecPATH_DRY (no way to deliver)
tef / tel / tem / terNot applied: malformed, rejected locally, or to retry. No fee, not in the ledger.temBAD_AMOUNT

SUCCESS DOES NOT MEAN "ALL OF IT ARRIVED"

A payment can show tesSUCCESS and deliver a tiny fraction of its stated amount. Lesson 9 explains how and what to check instead.

KEY IDEAOnly a result in a validated ledger is final. Read the metadata, not the request.

4 · Tokens, issuers and trust lines

ABOUT 6 MINUTES

Besides XRP, the ledger carries tokens (also called issued currencies or IOUs): digital dollars and euros, gold, tokenized treasury bills, shares. Every token is created by an issuer, an account that promises the token is backed by something. Stablecoins such as Ripple's RLUSD, Braza's BBRL, Schuman's EURØP and Novatti's AUDD are all issued this way on the XRPL.

Trust lines

To hold a token you open a trust line to its issuer with a TrustSet transaction. A trust line is a two-way link between you and the issuer that records a limit (the most you are willing to hold) and your balance. Each trust line you own counts toward your reserve.

Your account— trust line (limit 10,000 USD, balance 2,500) —Issuer rvYAf…

Currency codes do not prove anything

A token is identified by two things together: its currency code (a 3-character code like USD, or a 40-character hex code for longer names) and its issuer address. Anyone can issue a token called USD. The code is a label; the issuer is the identity. Scammers exploit this by sending look-alike tokens to people's accounts.

How much is out there

An issuer's obligations are the total of its tokens held by others, readable with the gateway_balances call. That is the real circulating supply per currency, and it is what NOSHASHI reports as outstanding.

THINK OF IT LIKE

A gift card. It is only worth something because the shop that issued it promises to honour it, and the shop printed the rules on the back. A card with the same name from a different shop is a different card.

KEY IDEAAsk "who issued this?" before "what is it called?". The issuer address is the token's identity.

5 · What an issuer can still do to your tokens

ABOUT 8 MINUTES · THE MOST IMPORTANT LESSON

Issuers can turn on powers, stored as flags on their account and settings on each trust line. They are public, but almost no wallet shows them. They decide whether a token is really yours.

6 · Who really controls an account

ABOUT 6 MINUTES

An account can be signed for in three ways, and more than one can be active at once:

  • Master key: the original key pair. It can be disabled with lsfDisableMaster, but only if another way to sign exists.
  • Regular key: a second key the account nominates. Useful for keeping the master key offline; it can be changed or removed.
  • Signer list (multi-signature): up to 32 signers, each with a weight. A transaction is valid when the weights of those who signed add up to the quorum.

Worked example: counting a quorum

A TREASURY WITH FIVE SIGNERS

Weights: Alice 3, Bob 2, Carol 2, Dan 1, Eve 1. Quorum: 5.

Alice and Bob together have 3 + 2 = 5, enough. So only two people are needed, not "three of five" as the headcount might suggest. NOSHASHI's Control Surface calculates this minimum number of signers from the weights.

And if the master key is still enabled, one person holding it can bypass the signer list completely. NOSHASHI flags this as a master-key bypass.

Blackholing

An issuer that disables its master key, sets its regular key to an address whose private key cannot exist (such as rrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrBZbvji), and has no signer list is blackholed. Nobody can ever sign for it again: its settings and supply are frozen in time, for better and worse.

KEY IDEACount weights, not people, and check whether a master key can skip the whole process.

7 · The DEX and order books

ABOUT 8 MINUTES

The XRPL has had a built-in exchange, the DEX, since 2012. Anyone can post an offer with OfferCreate, for example "sell 100 USD.Bitstamp for 190 XRP". All offers for one pair form an order book: bids on one side, asks on the other. The best prices meet in the middle; the average of the best bid and best ask is the mid price, and the gap is the spread.

When a new offer matches an existing one, they cross and trade. Token-to-token trades can be auto-bridged through XRP when that gives a better price.

Listed depth versus funded depth

Here is the catch that most tools miss. An offer stays on the book even after its owner no longer has the funds to fill it. It is still listed, but it cannot trade. These are unfunded offers. So:

  • Listed depth = everything shown on the book.
  • Funded depth = only what owners can actually deliver.

The ledger reports this itself. When you ask a server for a book with book_offers, an offer whose owner cannot cover it comes back with an extra field, taker_gets_funded, showing how much they really have. Adding up the listed amounts overstates the market.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Three offers to sell a token, each listing 10,000. The first owner holds 10,000, the second 500, the third 0. Listed depth: 30,000. Funded depth: 10,500. If you tried to sell 20,000 into this book, most of it would not fill.

KEY IDEAListed depth is an advert. Funded depth is what you can actually get out at.

8 · AMM pools

ABOUT 5 MINUTES

Since the AMM amendment (XLS-30), the XRPL also has automated market makers: pools that hold two assets, for example XRP and a stablecoin, and trade against anyone at a price set by the ratio of the two balances. The DEX routes trades through both order books and pools, whichever is better.

  • Liquidity providers deposit both assets and receive LP tokens, a share of the pool.
  • Every swap pays a trading fee (up to 1%) that goes to the pool. LP token holders vote on the fee, weighted by how many LP tokens they hold.
  • The auction slot is a seat anyone can bid LP tokens for. Its holder trades at a heavily discounted fee for a period, which rewards active traders who keep the pool's price in line.
  • A pool whose asset is frozen by its issuer can be frozen too.

NOSHASHI's Pool Governance screen shows who votes the fee, what share of LP tokens those votes represent, and who holds the auction slot, because a pool whose fee is set by one large holder behaves differently from one with a spread of voters.

KEY IDEAA pool's fee and discounts are governed by whoever holds the most LP tokens. Check who that is.

9 · Payments in depth

ABOUT 7 MINUTES

Partial payments

A Payment's Amount field says how much should arrive. But if the sender sets the partial payment flag (tfPartialPayment), the Amount becomes only a maximum. The payment can deliver far less and still finish with tesSUCCESS.

The authoritative figure is delivered_amount in the transaction's metadata. The XRPL's own documentation tells applications to credit that. Exchanges that credited Amount instead have been drained: attackers sent "large" payments that delivered almost nothing and withdrew the difference.

A REAL CASE NOSHASHI MEASURED · 28 AUGUST 2026

Amount: 999,332.87 LRC. delivered_amount: 3,958.64 LRC, about 0.4%. Result: tesSUCCESS. Any system crediting the stated figure would over-credit by roughly 250 times. In the same sample, 3 of 223 consecutive payments carried the partial-payment flag.

Other payment tools

PathsRoutes that let a payment convert currencies on the way, through order books and pools.

KEY IDEACredit what was delivered, never what was asked for.

10 · NFTs and the rights issuers keep

ABOUT 4 MINUTES

The XRPL supports native NFTs (non-fungible tokens, XLS-20): unique tokens for art, tickets, deeds or memberships. Each one has a 64-character NFTokenID, and the ID itself encodes the rules the issuer set when minting:

  • Burnable: the issuer can destroy it, even while you own it.
  • Transferable: if not set, you can only sell it back to the issuer.
  • Only XRP: it can only be sold for XRP.
  • Mutable: the issuer can change what its URI points at, so the picture or document can change after you buy it.
  • Transfer fee: the issuer takes up to 50% of every resale.

Because these rules are inside the ID, NOSHASHI's Token Rights screen can decode them without even contacting the ledger.

KEY IDEAOwning an NFT does not mean the issuer is done with it. Its ID tells you what they kept.

11 · Amendments: how the rules change

ABOUT 4 MINUTES

The XRPL's rules are changed by amendments. A new feature is written into the server software, then validators vote. It switches on only when it keeps at least 80% support for two weeks in a row. Servers that have not upgraded when an amendment activates become amendment blocked and stop following the ledger.

Many features in this course arrived this way: AMM (XLS-30), Clawback (XLS-39), NFTs (XLS-20), Credentials (XLS-70), Permissioned Domains (XLS-80) and Deep Freeze (XLS-77). "XLS" numbers are the public proposals behind them. NOSHASHI's Domain Grid lists which amendments are enabled.

KEY IDEANew rules need a supermajority for two weeks. What is enabled today is readable from the ledger.

12 · Compliance and regulation, made simple

ABOUT 8 MINUTES

Compliance means following the laws that apply to moving money. For anyone handling XRPL assets professionally, it comes down to two questions: am I allowed to move this, and could I actually get out of it? The first is law and policy. The second is market reality.

The words you will meet

  • KYC (Know Your Customer): verifying who a customer is.
  • AML (Anti-Money-Laundering): rules and monitoring to stop criminal money.
  • Sanctions screening: checking that no party is on a government blocklist. Wallet-analytics vendors do this by studying behaviour.
  • Travel Rule (FATF Recommendation 16): when a transfer between regulated providers is above a threshold, the sender's and receiver's details must travel with it. FATF suggests USD/EUR 1,000; the US uses USD 3,000; the EU applies it with no minimum. NOSHASHI lets you set your jurisdiction's threshold and tells you when a transfer is in scope.
  • VASP / CASP: a virtual (or crypto) asset service provider, the regulated business the rules apply to.
  • MiCA: the EU's crypto-asset regulation, which governs stablecoin issuers such as those issuing euro e-money tokens.

What screening misses

Behaviour screening asks "is this counterparty risky?". It cannot see issuer configuration. A perfectly clean holder can be holding a token whose issuer can freeze it or claw it back. That is a property of the asset, not of the person, and it lives in the flags from lesson 5. NOSHASHI reads both sides.

Compliance built into the XRPL

  • Credentials (XLS-70): an issuer of credentials (say, a KYC provider) can attach a verifiable credential to an account. It can be checked without revealing the underlying personal data, which is called selective disclosure.
  • Permissioned domains (XLS-80): a defined area where only accounts holding the right credentials can take part.
  • Counterparty concentration: how much of your activity goes to a few parties, measured with the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI): square each party's percentage share and add them up. 10,000 means one party has everything.

KEY IDEAKnow who you deal with, know what the asset allows, and keep evidence you can show later.

13 · What NOSHASHI is

ABOUT 4 MINUTES

NOSHASHI is a desktop app for macOS, Windows and Linux that reads the live XRP Ledger and answers the two compliance questions about any account, token, pool or payment, from the same ledger reading:

  1. Am I allowed to move this? Issuer powers, credentials, permissioned domains, Travel Rule scope, counterparty provenance and your own policy rules.
  2. Could I actually get out of it? Funded order-book depth, AMM pools, and how concentrated the holders are.

Other tools answer one question or the other: compliance vendors rarely read the order book, and market terminals rarely read the flags. NOSHASHI reads both from the same WebSocket in the same second, and reports them as one result.

14 · How NOSHASHI reads the ledger

ABOUT 5 MINUTES

  • Validated only. It reads validated ledgers from mainnet, the real XRP Ledger. Nothing is estimated, simulated or backfilled.
  • Four servers, compared. It queries four public servers by name. If they disagree, the disagreement is shown as a finding, never averaged away.
  • It says when it cannot tell. Where a figure cannot be established (for example, too little of a supply could be read to measure concentration), the app withholds it and says so, instead of showing zero.
  • No keys, no custody. It never asks for a secret key, never signs, never moves money. It only reads public data.
  • Stamped and fingerprinted. Every result carries the ledger index it was read at and a SHA-256 digest: a fingerprint of the exact result. Change one character and the digest changes completely.
  • Local first. Your adjudication history (up to 10,000 verdicts) is stored on your device. The AI agent runs on your device by default.
  • Self-verifying. The app can hash its own program file, so you can confirm you are running the published build.

The calls it makes are standard XRPL server methods, listed in Under the hood, so anyone can reproduce a reading with their own tools.

16 · Verdicts, checks and receipts

ABOUT 6 MINUTES

When NOSHASHI adjudicates something, it runs a set of checks, each with a severity. The overall verdict follows from them:

GOEvery check passed. Nothing on the ledger stands in the way.
HOLDA warning-level check did not pass, or a check needs review. A person should look before moving.
NO-GOA blocking check failed. Do not move until it is resolved or formally excepted.

Each individual check reports one of five states:

PASSRun and satisfied.
FAILRun and not satisfied.
REVIEWNeeds a human decision.
INSUFFICIENT_DATAThe ledger did not give enough to decide. An abstention, never a pass.
NOT_APPLICABLEThe check does not apply here.

The receipt

Every verdict produces a cryptographic receipt: what was checked, the ledger index, the rules version, each check's outcome and a SHA-256 digest over all of it. Copy it into your records. Anyone who later recomputes the digest over the same contents gets the same value; if anything was altered, they will not.

Your policy

Checks can be tuned by your organisation's policy: your thresholds and required credentials. When a verdict is shown under a policy it says so, and a verdict shown as "raw ledger facts, no policy applied" means only the built-in checks ran.

KEY IDEARead the verdict, then open the checks: every result names the exact rule that decided it.

18 · For organisations and developers

ABOUT 5 MINUTES

Organisation policy with four-eyes approval

An organisation can share one policy. A change is saved as a draft, can be simulated against past verdicts to see what it would have changed, and goes pending when submitted. A second person must approve it before it is active (four-eyes). Every step is logged.

Exceptions and evidence requests

When a NO-GO must be overridden for a good reason, a user requests an exception. A reviewer can approve, reject, or request more evidence first. The decision and its reasons are kept with the verdict.

Investigations

An investigation (a case) collects verdicts, readings and notes about one issuer or counterparty. Its log is hash-chained: each entry includes the fingerprint of the one before, so entries cannot be quietly edited or removed. Cases can be shared across an organisation.

Webhooks and the Compliance API

Webhooks send signed notifications of events (for example, a policy activated) to your own systems. The Compliance API offers read access to verdicts and readings for integration; see the API docs.

19 · The Agent and the Observer

ABOUT 4 MINUTES

The Agent is an AI assistant built into the app. By default it runs on your own device through a local model (Ollama or LM Studio), so nothing leaves your machine. You can connect a remote provider with your own API key instead.

  • Compliance mode: ask about a reading, a verdict or a rule, and it answers from what the app actually measured.
  • Support mode: help with using the app, with human escalation when needed.
  • Observer mode: watches the issuers and wallets you care about and raises alerts when something changes, such as an issuer flag flipping. One click opens an investigation with the evidence attached.
  • Governance: rules for what the agent may and may not do.

USE IT WISELY

The agent explains; the ledger decides. Always confirm important points against the readings and receipts themselves.

Questions people ask

Do I need to own crypto or connect a wallet?

No. NOSHASHI only reads public ledger data. It never asks for a secret key and cannot move funds.

Does a GO mean something is safe?

No. GO means nothing on the ledger stands in the way under the checks that ran. It says nothing about the business behind an issuer, prices or the future.

Why is a well-known issuer NO-GO?

Usually because one key can sign for it, or it keeps freeze rights. Those are normal for many regulated issuers. The verdict states the fact so you decide knowingly; it is not an accusation.

Why does a figure say "insufficient data" instead of a number?

Because the ledger did not return enough to support one. NOSHASHI will not guess and will not show zero for "unknown".

Can I check a reading myself?

Yes. Ask any XRPL server for the same data at the same ledger index, or re-run the free certificate. Recomputing the digest over the same contents gives the same value.

Where is my data stored?

Your verdict history and the local agent stay on your device. Account, plan and organisation features use the NOSHASHI service. The website certificate stores nothing.

Is this financial or legal advice?

No. It reports ledger facts and what they imply. Decisions remain yours and your advisers'.

Under the hood

FOR THE CURIOUS

How NOSHASHI is built

The desktop app uses Tauri 2: a Rust core with a React and TypeScript interface, packaged natively for macOS, Windows and Linux, with an iOS build in progress. It connects to public XRPL servers directly over WebSocket and compares four of them. Accounts, plans and organisation features (policies, exceptions, investigations, webhooks, the Compliance API) run on Supabase edge functions with row-level security. This website is a static site on Vercel with a few server functions, such as the free certificate. Releases are built by GitHub Actions and signed, and installed copies update themselves.

The ledger calls it uses
  • ledger: the latest validated ledger index.
  • account_info: an account's balance, flags, sequence and regular key.
  • account_objects: signer lists, escrows, offers and other owned objects.
  • account_lines: trust lines, limits, balances and freeze state.
  • gateway_balances: an issuer's obligations per currency.
  • book_offers: order books, including taker_gets_funded.
  • amm_info: pool balances, fee, votes and auction slot.
  • tx and account_tx: transactions and their metadata, including delivered_amount.
How the authority verdict is computed

Six checks: freeze permanently surrendered (warning), issuance not globally frozen now (blocking), holding does not require issuer permission (warning), no single key controls the issuer (blocking), no issuer transfer fee (warning), and supply concentration (warning; an abstention when the supply was not walked). Any failed blocking check gives NO-GO; otherwise any failed warning gives HOLD; otherwise GO. The certificate records the ledger index, the rules version and a SHA-256 digest over the canonical checks.
