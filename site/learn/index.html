PART 1 · THE XRP LEDGER

1 · The ledger in one minute

ABOUT 3 MINUTES

The XRP Ledger (XRPL) is a public database that thousands of computers keep identical copies of. It records who owns what, and every change to that. Anyone can read it, and nobody can quietly edit it.

Every few seconds the network agrees on a new version, called a ledger, numbered one after another. Once enough trusted servers (called validators) agree on it, it becomes a validated ledger and cannot change. NOSHASHI only reads validated ledgers, and every reading carries the ledger index (its number) so anyone can check the same moment later.

THINK OF IT LIKE

A shared notebook that the whole world can read, where a new page is added every few seconds and old pages can never be torn out.

Accounts and XRP

An account is an address on the ledger. Addresses start with the letter r, for example rvYAfWj5gh67oV6fW32ZzP3Aw4Eubs59B. Whoever holds the account's secret key can sign transactions for it.

XRP is the ledger's own currency. Every account must keep a small amount of XRP locked as a reserve, which stops people filling the ledger with junk accounts.

KEY IDEAThe ledger is public and final. Anything NOSHASHI tells you, you could check yourself at the same ledger index.

2 · Tokens and issuers

ABOUT 4 MINUTES

Besides XRP, the ledger carries tokens: digital dollars, euros, gold, shares and more. A token is created by an issuer, an account that promises the token is worth something, for example a stablecoin company promising one token equals one US dollar.

To hold a token you first open a trust line to its issuer. A trust line is a one-to-one link that says "I accept up to this much of your token." Your balance of that token lives on the trust line.

THINK OF IT LIKE

A gift card. The card is only worth something because the shop that issued it promises to honour it, and the shop set the rules printed on the back.

That is the key point: a token is only as good as its issuer, and the issuer sets rules on it. Two tokens can both be called "USD" and be issued by completely different accounts. The name (the currency code) is not owned by anyone; the issuer address is what matters.

KEY IDEAAlways ask "who issued this?" before "what is it called?"

3 · What an issuer can still do to your tokens

ABOUT 5 MINUTES

Issuers can switch on powers, called flags, on their account. These powers are public. Most wallets never show them. They are the first thing NOSHASHI reads.

POWERWHAT IT MEANS FOR A HOLDER
FreezeThe issuer can freeze your trust line so you cannot send the token on.
Global freezeThe issuer freezes every holder at once. The token stops moving for everyone.
Deep freezeA stronger freeze (amendment XLS-77): a frozen holder can neither send nor receive the token.
No FreezeThe issuer has permanently given up freezing. This can never be undone, so holders are protected from freezes.
Require AuthYou need the issuer's permission before you can hold the token at all.
ClawbackThe issuer can take tokens back out of your account. It must be switched on before any tokens exist, so it is a permanent feature of that token.
Transfer feeThe issuer takes a percentage every time holders send the token to each other. The issuer can change it.

Who controls the issuer?

An account can be signed for by its master key, by a regular key (a second key it nominates), or by a signer list (multi-signature: several people must agree). If one single key can sign for an issuer, one person or one stolen key can use every power above. An issuer that has disabled its master key and set its regular key to an address nobody controls is blackholed: nobody can ever act on it again.

None of this is good or bad by itself. A regulated stablecoin is usually expected to keep freeze and clawback. What matters is that you know before you hold it.

KEY IDEAA token can be yours on paper and still be frozen or taken back. The rules are on the ledger if you read them.

4 · Trading on the ledger

ABOUT 5 MINUTES

The XRPL has a built-in exchange, the DEX (decentralised exchange). People post offers ("I will sell 100 USD for 190 XRP"). All the offers for a pair form an order book. The amount on offer is called depth.

Here is the catch. An offer stays on the book even after the person who posted it no longer has the money to fill it. Those are unfunded offers. So the depth you see (listed depth) can be much bigger than the depth anyone could actually trade against (funded depth). The ledger reports this: book_offers returns a field called taker_gets_funded when an owner cannot cover their offer.

A REAL MEASUREMENT · LEDGER 106,850,266 · 8 SEP 2026

On the Bitstamp USD book, funded depth within 10% of the mid price matched listed depth (1.0×). On the SOLO book, 94.7% of listed depth came from offers nobody could fill. The median across six pairs was 4.4×. Method notes: the 10% band is a parameter we chose, AMM pools are left out, and the ledger returns at most 60 price levels. Re-measure before quoting; books change constantly.

AMM pools

An AMM (automated market maker, amendment XLS-30) is a pool holding two assets that trades automatically at a price set by the pool's balance. People who add money get LP tokens. LP token holders vote on the pool's trading fee, and one account can win the auction slot, which gives it discounted trading.

KEY IDEAListed depth is an advert. Funded depth is what you can actually get out at.

5 · Payments that say more than they deliver

ABOUT 3 MINUTES

A payment on the XRPL has an Amount field. If the sender sets the partial payment flag, the Amount becomes a maximum, not a promise. The payment can still show success (tesSUCCESS) while delivering only a fraction.

The number to trust is delivered_amount, in the transaction's result. XRPL's own documentation says to credit that. Systems that credited the Amount instead have been drained this way.

A REAL CASE NOSHASHI MEASURED · 28 AUG 2026

Amount said 999,332.87. delivered_amount said 3,958.64, about 0.4%. The transaction succeeded. In the same sample, 3 of 223 consecutive payments carried the partial-payment flag.

Two more payment words: a destination tag is a number that tells a shared account (like an exchange) which customer a payment is for, and escrow locks XRP until a time or condition is met.

KEY IDEACheck what a payment delivered, not what it asked for.

6 · Compliance words, made simple

ABOUT 4 MINUTES

  • Compliance: following the laws and rules that apply to moving money.
  • KYC / AML: Know Your Customer and Anti-Money-Laundering: checking who people are and stopping criminal money.
  • Travel Rule (FATF Recommendation 16): above a threshold, sender and receiver details must travel with a transfer between providers. NOSHASHI tells you when a transfer is in scope for your jurisdiction's threshold.
  • Amendment: a change to the XRPL's rules. It only switches on after most validators vote for it for two weeks.
  • Permissioned domains (XLS-80): areas of the ledger where only approved accounts can take part.
  • Credentials (XLS-70): on-ledger proofs that an account has passed a check (for example KYC), which can be shown selectively.
  • Counterparty: whoever is on the other side of your transaction.
  • Concentration: how much of something a few holders own. NOSHASHI measures it with the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI).

KEY IDEACompliance on the XRPL is two questions: am I allowed to move this, and could I actually get out of it?

PART 2 · THE NOSHASHI APP

7 · What NOSHASHI is

ABOUT 3 MINUTES

NOSHASHI is a desktop app (macOS, Windows and Linux) that reads the live XRP Ledger and answers two questions about any account, token or payment, from the same ledger reading:

  1. Am I allowed to move this? The compliance side: issuer powers, credentials, Travel Rule, counterparty checks.
  2. Could I actually get out of it? The market side: funded order-book depth, AMM pools, concentration.

It reads validated mainnet only. Nothing is estimated or made up: where a figure cannot be established, the app says so instead of showing zero. Every result carries the ledger index and a SHA-256 digest, a fingerprint anyone can recompute to prove the record was not changed.

STATUS

NOSHASHI is in beta (version 1.0.9). Every build is labelled beta. It is for information, not financial or legal advice.

8 · Getting started

ABOUT 3 MINUTES

  1. Go to Downloads and pick your system: .dmg for Mac (Apple Silicon or Intel), .msi or .exe for Windows, .deb, .rpm or .AppImage for Linux.
  2. Install and open it. The landing screen shows a live view of the ledger; continue into the console.
  3. The left sidebar holds every screen, grouped into sections: Overview, Adjudication, Markets & Exposure, Treasury & Issuance, Record, Intelligence, Public and Growth.
  4. Start with Check an Address (free): paste any address that starts with r and read what the ledger says about it.
  5. Updates arrive in the app under Settings › Updates, so you install once.

No crypto is needed to use it. You never have to connect a wallet or share a secret key; NOSHASHI only reads public data.

9 · Reading a verdict

ABOUT 3 MINUTES

When NOSHASHI checks something, it gives one overall verdict, and a result for every individual check that led to it.

GOEvery check passed. Nothing on the ledger stands in the way.
HOLDSomething needs a person to look before moving. Not a no, a "check first".
NO-GOA blocking check failed. Do not move until it is resolved.

Each individual check shows one of five states:

PASSThe check was run and was satisfied.
FAILThe check was run and was not satisfied.
REVIEWThe result needs a human decision.
INSUFFICIENT_DATAThe ledger did not give enough to decide. This is an abstention, never a pass.
NOT_APPLICABLEThe check does not apply to this case.

Every verdict names the exact rule that decided it, and comes with a receipt: the ledger index, the rules version and a SHA-256 digest.

Under the hood

FOR THE CURIOUS · OPEN ANY PANEL

How NOSHASHI is built

The desktop app is built with Tauri 2: a Rust core with a React and TypeScript interface. It talks to public XRPL servers directly over WebSocket and checks four of them against each other. Accounts, plans and shared organisation features (policies, investigations, webhooks) run on Supabase edge functions. This website is a static site on Vercel with a few server functions, such as the free certificate.

The ledger calls it reads

account_info (flags and keys), account_lines (trust lines), gateway_balances (an issuer's obligations per currency), book_offers (order books, with taker_gets_funded), amm_info (pools), tx (a transaction and its delivered_amount) and ledger (the validated ledger index).

For organisations

Organisation policies with a pending state and four-eyes approval (two people must agree), exceptions with an evidence request step, shared investigations with a hash-chained case log, signed webhooks for events, and a read-only Compliance API.

Release history

0.1–0.3: first public builds. 1.0.4–1.0.7: the gate, receipts, policy and investigations. 1.0.8: the Observer, one-click issuer investigation, five-state checks, evidence requests and the live garden. 1.0.9: screens that clipped now scroll, and groundwork for iOS.
