Rippling Exposure API · new · free to try

Can someone swap your tokens without your signature?

On the XRP Ledger, an account that holds one currency on two trust lines (USD from two issuers, say) can have value moved from one line to the other by anyone's payment, at par, unless it has set NoRipple. Nothing is stolen outright: the account simply ends up holding a different issuer's token than the one it chose. This check reads every trust line at one validated ledger and tells you which currencies can be moved, how much, and the exact unsigned transactions that close it.

A public address only. Never enter a secret, seed or recovery phrase.

    WHO NEEDS IT

    Exchanges and custodians whose hot or cold wallets hold the same currency from several issuers; treasuries that hold stablecoins from more than one issuer; wallets that want to warn users before an unwanted swap; and issuers, whose own accounts must ripple (the check flags an issuer with DefaultRipple off, which stops holders paying each other).

    WHAT IT CHECKS

    NoRipple on this account's side of each line, the value held on each open line, and the room left under each line's limit, because rippling can only move value into a line that has room. Quality settings are returned with every line. Pages of up to 2,000 lines are read at the same ledger as the account itself.

    The API

    curl -s "https://www.noshashi.app/api/v1/rippling?account=rYourAccount…"

    Returns verdict (EXPOSED, CLEAR, INCOMPLETE or UNRESOLVED), exposures per currency with movable_at_par and the lines at risk, findings about DefaultRipple, unsigned fixes, the validated ledger_index and server, and a SHA-256 receipt. Free and rate limited per caller. Monitoring many accounts, or an alert when a new open line appears: talk to us.

    Research note: how common is it?

    On 20 November 2026 we read the trust lines of twelve accounts drawn from the largest holders on the first page of Bitstamp's USD issuer. Five could have a currency moved between their own lines at par, from 2 to 102 units each. Seven could not: several held the same currency on many open lines, but none of those lines had room left under its limit, so nothing could ripple in. Holding a currency on several open lines is not, on its own, exposure; room to receive is what makes it so. No account is named here; anyone can repeat the reading with the check above.
