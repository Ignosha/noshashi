### Meeting questions: clear answers and proof required
The user previously practiced *simulated* questions about differentiation, buyer, evidence and readiness. These are not actual verbatim questions from Cyrille Bourdeaux.
1. **Different from explorers?** Explorer APIs expose blockchain transactions; NOSHASHI's proposed value is tying a transaction to internal intent, validated outcome, evidence and policy/version history. Benchmark the complete workflow against raw XRPScan/Bithomp and internal tools.
2. **Different from compliance providers?** Chainalysis KYT, TRM Labs and Elliptic provide real monitoring and screening. NOSHASHI must prove an additional XRPL operational-evidence workflow instead of duplicating AML intelligence.
3. **Who buys?** Hypothesis: XRPL wallet/payments operators, tokenization issuers, custody/compliance operations teams. Interview budget owners before declaring fit.
4. **Why enterprise price?** Measured hours saved, fewer unsupported investigations, integration, service reliability, auditable evidence, security, and a named budget owner. No contract at high pricing until these are demonstrated.
5. **Does it stop fraud?** No. The read-only gateway reports corroborated transaction outcomes, not ownership, illicit intent, off-chain solvency or regulatory compliance.
6. **What if data is missing?** Return UNKNOWN/HOLD. A transaction-not-found error is NOT proof of failure; history may be unavailable.
7. **Is it enterprise-ready?** No. Authentication and quotas are local single-process research controls; tenant isolation, security assessment, audits, production ops, support, multi-source validation and deployment readiness are still incomplete.
8. **Why XRPL first?** Niche value in issuer restrictions, destination tags, partial payments, trust lines, settlement evidence and DEX liquidity. Test narrow workflows before expanding chains.
9. **Proof of demand?** No paid buyers proven. Offer scoped measurable pilot, not claimed testimonials.
10. **What ask?** A 15-minute problem interview; if pain exists, propose a four-week read-only design-partner pilot.

## Paid four-week pilot: commercial hypothesis
Proposed fee $5,000 subject to actual buyer interest, procurement and agreed scope.
- Week 1: confirm data access, goals, 20–50 authorized historical cases, current baseline and reviewer.
- Week 2: demonstrate read-only XRPL outcome + evidence coverage and UNKNOWN cases.
- Week 3: blind benchmark NOSHASHI against current workflow and alternatives.
- Week 4: jointly record findings, errors, investigation minutes, evidence completeness and actual ROI; choose stop or expansion.

Example economic sensitivity, NOT achieved savings: 100 investigations/month × 30 minutes saved × $100 fully-loaded hourly staff cost = $5,000/month ($60,000/year). This by itself does NOT justify a $150,000/year contract. A six-figure enterprise contract needs genuinely greater operational value, contracts, support, integrations, security, SLA, availability and buyer validation.

Pricing hypotheses only (not live offers): developer sandbox free; Team $99–$499/month; Growth $999–$2,999/month; Institutional $50k–$150k/year; large integrated Enterprise $200k+/year. No billing is enabled.

## Release and buyer-readiness gates
1. CI unit/integration/browser tests pass and historical validated XRPL result corroborates across two independently operated RPC providers; unknown cases never invent final outcomes.
2. Local gateway enforces strict input shape, auth, quotas, bounded memory/concurrency, no dynamic upstream URL, TLS, short timeouts, and no private key/signing paths.
3. Production **must add** multi-tenant identity/access, secret rotation, durable distributed limits, audit trails, retention, uptime/alerting, incident response, supply-chain security and independent security audit.
4. Before any paid launch: service-level commitments, legal/DPA review, on-chain/off-chain attestation limits, customer integration pilot and quantified ROI.
5. Claims in website, demo and outreach must distinguish concept, local prototype, independently corroborated evidence, and production service.
