# NOSHASHI Institutional Product Thesis and Design-Partner Research
Date: 2026-11-22. Status: research and hypotheses, not customer validation.

## The institutional buying question
**Why buy NOSHASHI instead of an explorer, custody vendor, compliance suite, or internal build?**

Proposed answer: an independent transaction-assurance and evidence layer that records the exact evidence, policy version, and decision context across preflight, approval drift, and post-incident reconstruction. NOSHASHI should complement, not replace, custody, AML screening, or regulated legal judgment. Its value must be demonstrated through reproducible evidence and reduced manual investigation/review time.

This responds to previously simulated XRPL Commons meeting questions about differentiation, buyer, willingness to pay, risk methodology, evidence, and product maturity. ## First buyer discovery questions
1. Tell us about your last failed, delayed, or disputed on-chain transfer. How long did reconstruction take and which systems did you use?
2. What evidence is missing when compliance approves a transfer but the on-chain state changes before execution?
3. What do your current custody, blockchain analytics, and reconciliation providers already solve?
4. How do you prove which exact policy version and ledger evidence supported a decision?
5. Who owns this problem and its budget? What would a four-week, read-only pilot need to prove?
6. What integration, data residency, security and procurement requirements would block a pilot?

## Quantified pilot proposal — subject to negotiation
- 4 weeks; proposed $5,000 paid pilot, only after buyer confirms scope and value.
- Input: 20–50 historical, authorized transaction cases or synthetic cases if sensitive data cannot be shared.
- Deliverables: reproducible transaction autopsy evidence and reason codes, evidence export, comparison against current manual workflow.
- Measures: median time to investigate, evidence completeness, unsupported/unknown rate, false claims of validation (target zero), staff hours saved.
- Success criteria agreed with customer before start; no promise of 30% savings without baseline.
- No transaction signing, no policy enforcement, no private keys, no automatic funds movement.

## Feature and price credibility gates
High-price institutional contracts require evidence of:
1. Independently checked ledger provenance and no false success; explicit unknown states.
2. Tenant isolation, scoped credentials, quotas, logs, retention, least privilege and external security testing.
3. Immutable/versioned policy evidence and independently verifiable signed receipts.
4. Customer-specific workflows and at least one genuine integration with custody/operations tools.
5. Measured operational ROI, uptime/SLA monitoring, incident response, contractual support.
6. Reproducible test fixtures, failover, replay/idempotency protection, signed webhooks where relevant.
7. Transparent limitations: on-chain signals cannot prove off-chain issuer solvency, legal rights or all fraud.
Pricing above $50k/year is a commercial hypothesis until customers validate value and controls.

## Positioning statement for meetings
'NOSHASHI is building an independent evidence and operational assurance layer for digital-asset transactions. Existing explorers show what happened on-chain; custody and compliance tools each address parts of the workflow. Our target is to connect transaction intent, validated ledger facts, policy context, changes after approval, and post-incident evidence into a reproducible decision record. We are XRPL-first, read-only today, and seeking scoped design partners to prove that this reduces investigation time and improves control evidence. We are not claiming production authorization, certified compliance, or customer-proven savings yet.'
