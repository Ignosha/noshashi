# NOSHASHI Continuity meeting brief

## What is it?

A desktop workspace that helps XRPL operations teams review changes, find exceptions and keep the evidence behind follow-up decisions.

## Who would buy it?

Issuer operations, custody teams and permissioned-domain operators are our first buyer hypotheses. A design partner would test it against a real recurring review.

## What works in the development build?

Change rehearsal links domain access to scheduled work. Issuer offboarding reads balances and permissions at a validated Mainnet ledger. Bridge and lending workspaces analyze supplied records. Findings can be exported.

## What makes it different?

Our focus is connecting ledger observations, supplied records and follow-up in one review, with explicit evidence gaps and replayable change reports. The advantage needs comparison with the buyer's current tools.

## What about competition?

XRPLHub advertises credential and eligibility APIs. Axelar provides bridge tracking. Custody and reconciliation vendors cover adjacent workflows. We must demonstrate the value of the combined review in a customer trial.

## Why would an institution pay?

It could reduce the time spent joining ledger observations, internal records and review evidence. We propose measuring review time and incorrect findings on the same cases the buyer handles today.

## What is the price?

Proposed $3,500 for one scoped assessment with two change options, or $10,000 for a four-week pilot with three cycles. Institutional is published at $4,000 monthly, with agreed delivery scope.

## What is still required?

Customer validation, durable shared review, hosted API access and native release verification. Supplied bridge, loan and customer-mapping records require independent verification.

## How can Commons help?

Introduce us to one issuer operations lead and one permissioned-domain operator willing to replay a recent review with us.
